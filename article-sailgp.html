SAILGP / BEYOND THE RACE

The cameras follow the foiling boats. But the real story of SailGP extends into the team bases, performance crews and organisations trying to change what the sport leaves behind.

In August 2026, the Baltic Sea off Sassnitz became a theatre of high-speed sailing. SailGP brought its international fleet to Germany, a race village filled with fans, and a competition that seemed determined to test the limits of the boats and the people aboard them.

The official result belongs to Australia. Tom Slingsby's Flying Roos won the Germany Sail Grand Prix on 23 August, their fifth event victory of the season. Canada finished second, Spain third. France registered a new SailGP speed record of 107.63 km/h over the weekend. Those numbers made the headlines — but they capture only one dimension of an unusually complex sporting environment.

The operation behind the spectacle

A SailGP event is a meeting point for athletes, engineers, shore crews, performance specialists, communications teams, sponsors and local organisations. What happens between races is as revealing as what happens at the marks.

For SHOWRUNNER's Beyond the Race editorial project, the guiding question is not simply who wins. It is what makes a high-performance team work — and how the larger ecosystem surrounding that team gets built.

The race is the visible outcome. The people and the system behind it are the story.

Different teams. Different cultures.

The home event was an opportunity to look beyond the global SailGP brand and into individual team identities. Germany's programme, led on the water by Erik Kosegarten-Heil, made a notable step forward during the event, recording two consecutive fleet-race wins.

Elsewhere in the paddock, different programmes connect competition with projects in conservation, education and access to sailing. These are not interchangeable branding exercises: each has its own people, partnerships and priorities. The editorial challenge is to investigate what actually happens behind the claims.

From the team base to the wider world

Beyond the Race is conceived as a continuing magazine series: one that follows the people around SailGP across the season, not only during the weekend when cameras are rolling. The subjects extend from sports psychology and team performance to youth pathways, environmental work, sponsorship and leadership.

That approach changes the way an event is covered. A team member encountered in Sassnitz can become part of a longer story, linked to another race, an organisation, a project and a future interview. Each connection makes the archive more useful.

What remains after the fleet leaves?

For a racing league built around speed, the longer-term questions are slower: What knowledge stays behind? Which youth programmes continue after the event? How do teams turn individual initiatives into durable opportunities? And how does the culture of each team develop over a season?

These are questions the series will continue to explore. The race results provide an opening chapter, not an ending.
